Over the past two weeks I re-ran, on the server, the per-choice scoring of Centaur on Psych-101 that we had done before. I also added the effect of different context windows on the answers. For comparison I added several Llama-3.2 models; Gemma is still being debugged.

For a single model and a single session we store three kinds of data. The first is what the model actually answered, which lets us see whether it understood the question but got it wrong, or did not understand it and answered at random. The second is the model's top 20 tokens and their probability distribution, which shows how confident the model was when it answered. The third is the probability the model gave to the task's correct answer.
The metrics reported so far are three: the NLL, the probability it converts to, and the ratio of the model's choice probability to the baseline's.

Based on these metrics, the following analyses were done

1. Under the full session, the trend of per-trial NLL across models
The result is that almost all of the drop in NLL falls in the first decile of the session and the curve is flat afterwards, with Centaur going from 0.91 to 0.76 and Llama-3.1 from 1.26 to 0.97

![A17 within-session trend](../outputs/analysis/trackp-greedy1tok-v1-8c072183d8d6/a17/within_session_decile.png)

2. Under different windows, the trend of NLL and the probability it converts to across models
The result is that one segment of history alone removes most of the gap, the NLL difference between Centaur and Llama-3.1 falling from 2.43 nat to 0.31 nat, with each further segment adding little

![A4 context-length curve](../outputs/analysis/trackp-greedy1tok-v1-8c072183d8d6/a4/a4_curve.png)

3. When the model did not answer in the required format, a classification of what it actually emitted
The result is that most of the format-violating readouts come from the human's own answer being multi-token, while the genuinely off-task ones number 175 for Centaur and 1,281 to 4,026 for the base models

4. Per task, a comparison of each model's answer distribution against the humans' real answer distribution
The result is that Centaur's answer distribution is the closest to the humans', its per-session TVD being 0.06 against 0.10 to 0.12 for the base models, and none of the six models' probability distributions degenerate into always naming the mode
